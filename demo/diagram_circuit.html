AND Query as Series Circuit

Query: "Sci-Fi films by Nolan" — Nolan fixed at V=1.0 (source), Sci-Fi fixed at V=0.0 (sink). Score = current through each movie.

How it works

The electric circuit analogy, step by step

1

The knowledge graph is a resistor network

Every entity (Nolan, Interstellar, Sci-Fi…) is a node. Every relationship (directed_by, has_genre…) is a resistor connecting two nodes. The resistance of each edge comes directly from the ontology: a high-weight relationship like directed_by = 0.9 is a low-resistance (high-conductance) wire — it passes signal easily. A weak relationship like has_tags = 0.2 is a high-resistance wire — it barely passes signal.

Conductance G = ontology weight  ·  Resistance R = 1/G

2

The query anchors are voltage sources

When you issue an AND query — "Sci-Fi films by Nolan" — you are clamping two nodes to fixed voltages and letting the rest of the network reach equilibrium. Nolan is the source: V = 1.0. Think of it as the positive terminal of a battery. Sci-Fi is the sink: V = 0.0. That is the negative terminal — the ground. Every other node's voltage is determined by Kirchhoff's laws: at equilibrium, the current flowing into each interior node equals the current flowing out.

Anchors = Dirichlet boundary conditions  ·  Solved via graph Laplacian L·V = s

3

Voltage = how close an entity is to each anchor

Interstellar is connected to Nolan via directed_by (G=0.9) and to Sci-Fi via has_genre (G=0.7). It sits between two voltage sources and its equilibrium voltage settles at V = 0.5625 — closer to Nolan (0.9 conductance) than to Sci-Fi (0.7 conductance), like a voltage divider.

Dark Knight is connected to Nolan via directed_by (G=0.9) but its other edge leads to Action — a genre with no path to Sci-Fi. With no second anchor to pull it down, Dark Knight's voltage floats to V = 1.0, identical to Nolan.

Voltage divider: V(entity) = G_source / (G_source + G_sink) when connected to both

4

Current = the score

Current only flows where there is a potential difference. An entity scores well if it sits on a conducting path between source and sink — carrying current from high voltage to low voltage. Interstellar: V = 0.5625, neighbors at V = 1.0 and V = 0.0 → potential difference exists → I = 0.39. Dark Knight: V = 1.0, same as Nolan → zero potential difference with its only live neighbor → I = 0. It is automatically excluded.

I(entity) = Σ G(e,j) × max(0, V(j) − V(e))  ·  score = current

→

Why this matters: no other tool does all three

SPARQL / graph query

Schema-grounded. Typed edges. But binary — a path either exists or it doesn't. No partial matches, no scoring.

Vector / embedding search

Continuous scoring. Handles partial similarity. But schema-free — no typed edges, no ontology, no multi-hop structure.

open-kgo SemanticField

Schema-grounded and continuous. Typed edges with weights. Multi-hop AND queries. Partial satisfaction scored, not binary.
